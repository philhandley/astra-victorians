Astra Victorians · pictures to look atQueen Victoria's bathing machine

This is the bathing machine Queen Victoria used at Osborne, her house by the sea on the Isle of Wight. She first went into the sea from it on 30 July 1847. It is 12 feet long: as long as a small car.

Not in the photo

The winch. No horse for the Queen. A man turned the handle of a winch, and its wire rope let the machine down the rails into the sea, then wound it back up.

The dipper. A strong helper who waited in the water to help bathers in, and sometimes dunked them under.

Did you know?

Victoria wrote in her diary that her first dip was delightful, until she put her head under the water.

After she died, her bathing machine was used as a hen house! It has been mended, and it stands by the beach at Osborne.

RoofIt slopes down on all four sides, in overlapping rows, so the rain runs off.

CanopyThe roof reaches out over the steps, so the Queen kept dry going down them.
3
Iron bracketsCurly scrolls of iron, painted green, hold up the canopy.
4
VerandaA little porch at the sea end. The Queen stepped out behind its curtains, so nobody on the beach could see her.
5
StepsFive white steps lead down into the sea. At the bottom of the rails, the lowest ones were under the water.
6
Changing roomA little green room where the Queen changed into her bathing dress. Inside was a real toilet with pipes.
7
WindowA small window high up lets in the light. The door and window had silver handles.
8
WheelsFour big wheels with flat iron tyres. Each one is about as tall as you are.
9
Stone railsThe wheels ran in grooves in stone rails, down a slipway into the sea. Pieces of the rails are still under it.
Spot the differenceMost bathing machines were pulled into the sea by a horse. The Queen's ran on stone rails and was let down by a winch. How many wheels has the horse's machine?
