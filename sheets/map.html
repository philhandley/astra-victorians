Astra Victorians · homeworkHow to make a map
10POINTS
1. Walk around it

Walk round the place with a grown-up. Look at what is where: what is next to what, and what is far apart.

2. Choose what to show

Pick 6 to 10 important things: doors, trees, the pond, the shed, your bed. A map does not need everything.

3. Draw the outline from above

Imagine you are a bird looking straight down. Draw the edge of the room or garden first, then the big things inside it.

4. Add symbols

Use a small, simple symbol for each thing. Use the same symbol every time you draw a tree, a door or a window.

5. Make the key

In the key, draw each symbol once and write what it means. A map reader uses the key to understand your map.

6. Compass and title

Add a compass with N for north at the top, and a title such as "A map of my garden". Done!

What is a bird's-eye view?

Maps are drawn from above, like a bird or a drone would see things. The top of a table is a rectangle; a round tree is a circle; a path is a wiggly line.

A Victorian map fact

While Queen Victoria was on the throne, the Ordnance Survey mapped every county of Britain in enormous detail. Cheshire was surveyed between 1870 and 1875, so every field, church, canal and railway around Wrenbury was drawn on a map with a key to the symbols, just like yours. This one shows Chester.
