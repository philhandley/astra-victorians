Astra Victorians · homeworkHow a real bathing machine worked
10POINTS
  1. You climb in at the beach end and shut the door.
  2. Inside, you change into your bathing costume while the machine is pulled down the sand, usually by a horse.
  3. In the water, the door at the sea end opens. You go down the steps into the sea, where nobody on the beach can see you.
  1. A strong helper called a "dipper" is there to hold you and dip you under the waves.
  2. Raise the little flag to tell the driver you are ready. Climb back in, dry off and get dressed while the machine is pulled back up the beach.
  3. Some machines had a canvas hood over the sea door, so the bather was hidden completely.

    Astra Victorians · homeworkA bathing machine today
    10POINTS

    Bathing machines never quite went away. In 2019 a seaside skincare company called Haeckels put a brand-new one on the sands at Margate, with a sauna inside.

    • A wooden hut on big oak wheels with a steel frame, built the way the Victorian ones were.
    • Inside is a wood-burning sauna. You get hot, then sprint into the cold sea a few metres away.
    • The steps fold up to become the door, and a waxed cloth awning rolls out over the front.
    • Twenty people helped build it: an architect, joiners, a sauna maker and a wheelwright who makes wooden wheels.
    • It was paid for by lots of people chipping in, and their names are engraved on the side. Anyone can use it for free.
    What would you put in a modern one?

    Source: Dezeen, "Haeckels' Victorian bathing machine warms up winter seaside dippers", 23 December 2019. Photos are on dezeen.com; the link is on the website.
